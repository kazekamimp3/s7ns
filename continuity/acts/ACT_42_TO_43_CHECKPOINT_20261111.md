## 6. TTS production rules

- Syntax: `Speaker (style: "short style"): spoken text`.
- Critical label rule: do not put “Wade (” or a character-name label before the text. The spoken text begins immediately after the colon and space, avoiding the engine reading “Wade” aloud.
- Split each act into three 8–10-minute parts, approximately 4,000–8,000 characters each, to avoid browser crashes.
- Use documented tags only, such as `<chuckle>`, `<cackle>`, `<snort>`, `<giggle>`, `<sigh>`, `<pant>`, `<gasp>`, `<groan>`, `<short pause>`, `<long pause>`, and `<throat-clearing>`.
- Laugh tags go after punchlines, except a snort may precede a joke.
- No square-bracket stage directions.
- Profanity dial at maximum. Target at least 30 hard swears in Wade’s own narration per 30-minute act. No soft substitutions such as “heck,” “darn,” “freaking,” or “crap.”
- One punchline per sentence. Avoid adjective stacking.
- Maintain high plot momentum: set up the joke, land it, trigger the consequence. Characters must not remain in one puddle for ten minutes.
- Use short, one- or two-line deadpan baseline blocks to let chaos drop into silence.
