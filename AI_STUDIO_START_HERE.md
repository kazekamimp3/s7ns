## Mandatory episode-generation firewall

These checks are required BEFORE every episode draft and AGAIN before delivery. They are not optional style preferences.

### A. Build an episode-specific presence lock

Before writing, retrieve the live current-state owner, active outline/selected chronology, latest scoped author directions, and the complete owners for every proposed participant. Build an internal list with:
- confirmed physically present at this episode's start;
- explicitly selected to enter during this episode, with the supported entry cause;
- remote/off-screen actors whose actions can affect events only through established access;
- excluded, dead, departed, unavailable, or merely historical actors.

The current Seven Sins roster is NOT an automatic scene roster. Membership does not prove presence, and a character appearing in an old episode, archive, source example, or crossover registry does not prove current availability. Only the episode's controlling outline plus current state and applicable author decisions authorize participation.

Never add a character to a scene to fill space, complete a group composition, create a joke, or because the character appeared in an earlier generated draft. Never turn a narrator's joke, stage direction, or recap into evidence that an event or presence occurred.

### B. Enforce status and identity before prose

- Check each participant's current alive/dead/departed/held status against live state and the latest complete owner. A dead character cannot appear alive, speak, move, or participate unless an authorized return event is already established in current continuity. Do not invent or imply a resurrection to make a scene work.
- Check exact name, pronouns, species, body configuration, speech permissions, relationships, and consciousness/body arrangement against the controlling complete owner.
- Mana is male and uses he/him/his only. He is a fully canine, quadrupedal, nonverbal blue-nose pit bull. No human dialogue, human subtitles, humanoid posture, or female pronouns.
- Chris and Catfish Billy are separate persons/consciousnesses sharing one exterior body. Never merge their identities or give them simultaneous separate bodies without an established event.
- Brian is not a current Seven Sins member. His current status is permanently dead, killed by Kaze. His historical living severed-head period does not authorize a present-day appearance. Do not place him in a current group scene or expedition.
- Do not infer membership, attendance, survival, revival, relationship, knowledge, equipment, or ability from a name appearing in a source.

If any required fact conflicts across sources, stop that affected portrayal and report the exact conflict. Do not silently choose the more convenient version.

### C. Keep narration separate from canon

The narrator's voice controls wording, rhythm, humor, point of view, and audience address only. It does not control world facts.

A Deadpool/Wade narration instruction does not authorize Wade to:
- invent who is present or what happened;
- resurrect, recruit, relocate, or reassign anyone;
- change pronouns, species, speech ability, relationships, or character ownership;
- grant characters knowledge they did not acquire;
- overwrite a character's dialogue, personality, or agency;
- turn an outline possibility into an occurred event.

Keep each character's own speech and behavior faithful to their complete owner. Do not perform character impressions by changing another character's vocal identity. The narrator can be funny without making every sentence a punchline; character comedy must arise from the character, situation, and established voice.

### D. Run the release-blocking validation

After drafting, inspect the COMPLETE episode, not just a sample. Validate:
1. Every physically present character is authorized by the episode-specific presence lock.
2. Every character's current status permits the depicted action.
3. Pronouns, species, body, speech, and consciousness rules are correct.
4. No unselected future beat is presented as having happened.
5. No narrator joke or stage direction creates an event, participant, item, relationship, or knowledge.
6. Dialogue preserves each speaker's individual voice and agency.
7. The ending and any project-state update reflect only events actually depicted and authorized.

Any failure above blocks delivery. Repair the draft and recheck the entire episode. If the source needed to decide a check is unavailable, mark it unresolved and do not invent a pass.

A generated episode is not author-accepted merely because it contains a [PROJECT STATE UPDATE]. Keep prepared, delivered, provisional, and accepted states distinct.
